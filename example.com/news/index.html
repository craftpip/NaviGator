City Spent $4.2 Million on Fountain That Nobody Asked For, Records Show

By Sarah Chen and Marcus Webb · Published Aug 17, 2026 · Updated 3 hours ago

📸 Aerial photo of the Testville fountain construction site, Aug 2026

TESTVILLE — The city government spent $4.2 million on a decorative fountain in the new civic plaza — nearly three times its original budget — according to internal documents obtained by the Testville Times under a public records request.

The project, originally estimated at $1.5 million in the 2024 capital budget, ballooned after a series of change orders, contractor disputes, and what one city engineer described in an email as "scope creep disguised as value engineering."

Three former city officials and two contractors spoke to the Times on the condition of anonymity, describing a pattern of last-minute design changes pushed by council members who wanted the fountain to be "iconic" — a word that appears 17 times in the project's internal correspondence.

How the costs spiraled

The fountain's original design was a simple recirculating water feature with LED lighting. But between January and June 2025, the project underwent 14 formal change orders that added a programmable music-and-light show, imported Italian marble cladding, and a underground mechanical room larger than some city apartments.

The council's role

Internal emails show that three council members — whose names the Times is withholding pending further reporting — pushed for the upgrades in private meetings with the project manager. One email from March 2025 reads: "We need this to be the thing people photograph. If it's just a regular fountain, why bother?"

The council approved each change order individually, often with less than 48 hours of public notice. Under city rules, change orders under $100,000 do not require a full council vote — a threshold that critics say is too high for a project of this size.

Mayor Linda Torres, who was elected on a fiscal responsibility platform, declined to comment for this story. Her office provided a written statement saying the fountain "reflects the ambition of our community" and that "cost management is an ongoing process."

What happens now

The city's Inspector General has opened a preliminary review of the project. Separately, a group of residents has collected 2,400 signatures on a petition asking the council to halt the fountain's scheduled Sept. 15 grand opening pending an independent audit.

The fountain, which is 85% complete, currently sits dry in the middle of the civic plaza. Its mechanical room is finished, its Italian marble is installed, and its 200 LED lights are wired — but the water has never been turned on.

Testville High Wins State Robotics Championship for Third Consecutive Year

By Priya Sharma · Aug 16, 2026

The Testville High School robotics team, the Gears of Wrath, defeated 47 other teams at the state championship in Columbus this weekend. Their robot, "Sansa," completed the obstacle course in a record 47 seconds. The team's coach, retired engineer Tom Nakamura, said the win was "-validation that our after-school program works."

The school has won the state title every year since 2024, when a group of sophomores built their first robot from a kit and a pile of spare parts. The program now serves 62 students across three grade levels.

Environment

City Completes Solar Streetlight Rollout on River Avenue

By Mira Patel · Aug 15, 2026

The final 340 solar-powered streetlights went live on River Avenue this week, completing the city's $2.1 million pilot project six weeks ahead of schedule. Public Works Director James Okafor said the avenue is now 15% brighter at night and costs 40% less to illuminate than before.

The project, funded by a state green infrastructure grant, replaces aging sodium-vapor lights with programmable LED fixtures powered by rooftop solar panels. Each light stores enough energy to operate for three consecutive cloudy days.

"This is what responsible infrastructure looks like," Okafor said at a press conference Tuesday. "We're saving money, reducing emissions, and making the street safer — all at the same time."

The city plans to expand the program to three additional avenues in 2027, pending council approval of the capital budget.

Transport

New East Side Bridge Clears Final Structural Inspection

By Diego Ramos · Aug 14, 2026

Engineers signed off on the structural report for the East Side Bridge on Tuesday, clearing the way for the $38 million crossing to open to traffic next month. The bridge, which spans the river between Elm Street and Route 9, was originally supposed to open in October 2025.

The 11-month delay was caused by a combination of supply chain disruptions, a labor dispute, and — according to one inspector's report — "unexpected geological conditions" that required a redesigned foundation. The project is now $4.2 million over its original $33.8 million budget.

Community

Market Vendors Report Record Summer Crowds; Geese Take Credit

By Aisha Khan · Aug 13, 2026

Sunday foot traffic at the River Avenue farmers' market hit 18,200 visitors — a record since the market moved to its current location in 2019. Vendors attributed the surge to good weather, new street art installations, and the growing fame of the market's resident goose colony.

"People come for the produce and stay for the geese," said market manager David Chen. "We've had three food bloggers write about them this month alone." The geese, who nest under the market's main pavilion, have become an unlikely social media sensation, with a fan account that has 14,000 followers.

Business

Downtown Bakery Expands to Second Location After TikTok Goes Viral

By Leila Ahmed · Aug 12, 2026

Flour & Fold, the downtown bakery whose croissants went viral on TikTok in June, has signed a lease for a second location on Oak Street. Owner Maria Gonzalez said the shop, which opened in March 2025, has been selling out of croissants by 9am every day since a video of her laminating dough got 2.3 million views.

The new location will include a dedicated baking classroom and a coffee bar. Gonzalez plans to hire eight additional staff members and hopes to open by November.

Health

Free Health Screenings at Community Center This Saturday

By Health Desk · Aug 11, 2026

The Testville Community Health Center is offering free blood pressure, cholesterol, and glucose screenings this Saturday from 9am to 3pm at the downtown community center. No appointment is needed. The event is open to all residents regardless of insurance status.

"We're trying to catch problems early," said Dr. Anita Patel, the center's medical director. "High blood pressure is called the silent killer for a reason — you can feel fine and still be at risk." Last year's event screened 340 people and identified 28 individuals who were referred for follow-up care.

Heat Advisory Issued for Friday; City Opens Cooling Centers

By Weather Desk · Aug 10, 2026

The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for Friday, with temperatures expected to reach 97°F — the hottest day of the summer. The city will open cooling centers at the downtown library, the Eastside Community Center, and three senior centers.
