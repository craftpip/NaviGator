Follow the Money: Testville's $47M Transit Contract Went to a Company With No Employees

By Diego Ramos · Published Aug 12, 2026 · Updated Aug 14, 2026 · 18 min read

A months-long investigation by The Testville Times reveals that the city's largest-ever transit modernization contract was awarded to a shell company registered at a residential address — and the firm had no employees on payroll at the time of the bid.

The contract

On March 15, 2026, the Testville City Council approved a $47.2 million contract with TransitNow Solutions LLC to modernize the city's aging light rail system. The contract — the largest in the city's history — was presented as a done deal: the council voted 8–3 to approve it in a single reading, with no public comment period.

The vote took place at 11:47 PM on a Thursday. By then, most residents had gone home.

Key numbers: $47.2M contract · 8–3 council vote · 11:47 PM Thursday vote · Zero public hearings · 30-day bid window (standard is 60)

The company's sole listed manager, according to state incorporation records, is Michael R. Chen. Chen is not a transit engineer. His LinkedIn profile (since deleted) listed his previous job as "Regional Sales Manager" at a Testville car dealership.

The bid process

TransitNow was one of four bidders. The Times obtained the bid evaluation documents through a public records request. The evaluation scores were:

Red flag: TransitNow scored 92/100 on technical capability — higher than firms with decades of transit experience — despite having no completed projects, no engineering staff, and no office address.

The evaluation committee was composed of three people:

  • Director of Transportation — Sarah Kim (appointed by the Mayor)
  • Chief Financial Officer — Robert Patel (city employee, 22 years)
  • External consultant — James Wu, hired by the city specifically for this evaluation

The Times contacted all three. Kim and Patel declined to comment. Wu said only: "The evaluation was conducted according to the scoring rubric provided by the city."

The city's Ethics Office said it had no record of Wu's appointment and that external evaluators are typically selected from a pre-approved roster. Wu was not on the roster.

What happens next

The Testville City Council has scheduled a special meeting for August 18 to discuss the contract. Three council members who voted against the contract — Maria Santos, David Kim, and Aisha Khan — are calling for an independent audit.

The State Attorney General's office confirmed it has opened a preliminary inquiry into the procurement process.

Meanwhile, the $12.3 million remaining in the contract has not been drawn. The city's legal department is reviewing whether the contract can be suspended pending the audit.
