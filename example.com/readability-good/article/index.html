Testville Council Approves Solar-Powered Streetlights

By Mira Patel · Published 2026-08-05 · Updated 2026-08-06 · 6 min read

The council voted 11–2 to replace all 4,300 streetlights in the city with solar-powered units, a move officials say will cut the municipal electricity bill by 40%.

TESTVILLE — After six months of public consultation and a two-week pilot, the Testville City Council has formally approved a city-wide switch to solar-powered streetlights. The resolution passed 11–2 at Thursday's meeting, with the two dissenting members citing upfront costs.

How the program works

Each new unit combines a compact photovoltaic panel, a battery pack, and an LED lamp rated for 50,000 hours. The panel charges during the day and the lamp runs automatically from dusk to dawn. Sensors dim the lights to 60% output after midnight when traffic is low.

The city expects to install roughly 700 units per month, beginning with residential streets and finishing with the downtown commercial district. "Solar makes sense for a city that gets 300 sunny days a year," said Public Works director Lena Okafor.

The numbers

  • 4,300 streetlights to be replaced
  • $6.2 million total project cost
  • $720,000 in estimated annual electricity savings
  • Three-year expected payback period
  • Estimated 1,900 tonnes of CO2 avoided over 20 years

What residents think

Support was strongest in neighborhoods that participated in the pilot. River Avenue resident Omar Haddad, who lives beside one of the prototype lights, said the change was barely noticeable. "It's actually brighter than the old one, and I like that the city keeps the street well lit," he told the Chronicle.

"Solar makes sense for a city that gets 300 sunny days a year." — Public Works director Lena Okafor

What's next

Installation crews begin work in October, starting with the Hillcrest neighborhood. A progress dashboard will be published on the city website, and residents can report faulty lights through the existing 311 line. The program is expected to finish by late 2027.

Correction: An earlier version of this story said the payback period was two years. It is three years.
