Testville is a city in South Testland, situated on the banks of the Test River. It is the largest city in the province with a population of 310,000.

The city was founded in 1876 as a trading post. Its economy historically relied on textiles and timber; today it is known for renewable energy, a growing software sector, and its extensive network of public parks.

History

Testville was established in 1876 by settlers who used the river to float timber to downstream mills. The arrival of the railway in 1901 turned it into a regional hub. In 1948 the Testville Dam was completed, ending a series of destructive spring floods.

Geography

The city lies in a valley at 40 m above sea level. The Test River bisects the city from north to south. Testville receives about 950 mm of rainfall per year and 300 days of sunshine.

Demographics

As of the 2025 census, the population was 310,000, up from 288,000 in 2020. Median age is 34. The city is home to a large student population attending two universities.

Landmarks

  • Central Park — the oldest park, opened 1892
  • The Testville Dam — completed 1948, 62 m high
  • Old Textile Mill — converted to a museum in 2001
  • River Avenue Promenade — pedestrian street with markets
