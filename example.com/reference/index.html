Testville is a city in South Testland, situated on the banks of the Test River. It is the largest city in the province with a population of 310,000 as of the 2025 census. The city is a regional hub for renewable energy, software development, and higher education, hosting two universities and a polytechnic.[1]

The city was founded in 1876 as a trading post by settlers who used the Test River to float timber to downstream mills.[2] The arrival of the Testville–Northern Railway in 1901 transformed it into a regional commercial center. The Testville Dam, completed in 1948, ended a series of destructive spring floods and created Lake Testville, now a popular recreation area.[3]

The city is known for its extensive network of public parks — 230 covering over 40 km² — which has earned it the nickname "The Green City."[4] In 2025, Testville was named one of the top 10 most livable cities in the country by Urban Living Magazine.

History

Founding (1876–1900)

Testville was established in 1876 by James Whitfield and Mary Harlow, two settlers who recognized the strategic value of the Test River's confluence with the South Fork. The original settlement consisted of a timber mill, a general store, and a ferry crossing.[2]

The early economy relied on timber, agriculture, and river trade. By 1890, the town had grown to 2,400 residents with a school, a post office, and three churches.[5]

Railway era (1901–1945)

The arrival of the Testville–Northern Railway in 1901 marked the beginning of rapid industrialization. The railway connected Testville to the national freight network, enabling large-scale export of timber and agricultural products. The population doubled between 1900 and 1920, reaching 18,000.

During World War II, the Testville Dam provided hydroelectric power to the region's war effort. The city's population grew further with an influx of workers for the dam's construction and operation.

"The dam was not just infrastructure — it was the promise that the river, which had always taken from us, would now give back." — Testville: A History, Dr. Sarah Lin, 2004[3]

Post-dam development (1946–2000)

With flood control secured, Testville expanded rapidly. Key milestones:

  • 1952: University of Testville founded
  • 1964: Construction of the Central Ring Road
  • 1978: Designation as a Special Economic Zone
  • 1991: Opening of the International Technology Park
  • 1998: First light rail line opens

The 1990s tech boom attracted software companies, transforming Testville from an industrial town into a knowledge economy hub. The city's GDP grew by an average of 8.3% annually between 1991 and 2000.[6]

Geography

The city lies in a valley at 40 m above sea level, bounded by the Test Hills to the north and the South Plain to the south. The Test River bisects the city from north to south, with several tributaries feeding into it within city limits.

The city is home to a large student population attending two universities and a polytechnic, with approximately 45,000 enrolled students during the academic year.[7]

Economy

Testville's economy is diversified across technology, education, healthcare, and tourism. The city's GDP was approximately $18.6 billion in 2025, making it the third-largest economy in the province.[6]

Landmarks

  • Central Park — the oldest park, opened 1892, 2.4 km², includes a zoo and botanical garden
  • Testville Dam — completed 1948, 62 m high, hydroelectric capacity 180 MW, creates Lake Testville
  • Old Textile Mill — converted to a museum in 2001, houses the Museum of Industrial Heritage
  • River Avenue Promenade — 2.8 km pedestrian street with markets, cafés, and public art
  • Testville Cathedral — Gothic Revival, built 1889–1897, tallest spire in the province at 68 m
  • Tech Tower — 32-story office building, tallest structure in the city (142 m)
  • Whitfield House — birthplace of founder James Whitfield, now a heritage museum
  • Testville Stadium — 45,000-seat multi-purpose stadium, opened 2018

Parks and recreation

Testville operates 230 parks covering over 40 km², accounting for approximately one-third of the city's total area. The parks system is managed by the Testville Parks Department, which employs 340 staff and maintains 1,400+ trees across the network.

Transportation

Testville is served by the Testville–Northern Railway with two stations: Testville Central and Testville South. The city operates a light rail system (2 lines, 28 stations) and a bus network (14 routes).

  • Airport: Testville International Airport (TVL) — 12 km south of city center, 1.2M passengers/year
  • Rail: Testville–Northern Railway — hourly services to the capital (2h 15min)
  • Light rail: Line 1 (North–South), Line 2 (East–West), 15-minute frequency
  • Bus: 14 routes, 120 buses, free WiFi on all vehicles
  • Cycling: 85 km of dedicated bike lanes, city bike share program (1,200 bikes)
  • Road: Connected to the national highway network via the Central Ring Road (A4)

Education

Testville has 42 public schools (28 primary, 14 secondary) and 8 private schools. The city hosts two universities and a polytechnic, with a combined enrollment of approximately 45,000 students.

  • University of Testville — founded 1952, 22,000 students, 14 faculties, ranked in the top 50 nationally
  • South Testland Polytechnic — founded 1971, 8,000 students, emphasis on engineering and applied sciences
  • International University of Testville — founded 2008, 5,000 students, English-language curriculum

Culture

Testville has a vibrant cultural scene, with over 30 museums, galleries, and performance venues. Notable institutions include:

  1. Testville Museum of Art — 12,000+ works spanning 500 years; free admission on Sundays
  2. Museum of Industrial Heritage — housed in the Old Textile Mill; interactive exhibits on the city's manufacturing history
  3. Testville Symphony Orchestra — performs at the 1,800-seat Grand Hall
  4. Riverfront Amphitheater — 3,000-seat outdoor venue, summer concert series
  5. Heritage Square Farmers Market — Saturdays year-round, 120+ vendors

Annual events include the Testville Festival (June), the Rose Festival (July), and the Tech Conference (September), which draws 8,000 attendees from across the region.
